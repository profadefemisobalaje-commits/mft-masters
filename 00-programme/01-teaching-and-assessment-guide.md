# Teaching and Assessment Guide for Lecturers

> For the person delivering the course. Short, practical, and designed so a new lecturer can pick up a book and teach it next week.

---

## 1. The default session shape (2 hours + 1 hour practical)

| Time | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | **Check-in round** | One sentence each: "one thing that stayed with you from last week". Builds the relational field you will later teach about. |
| 10–25 min | **Retrieval quiz** | Five closed questions on last week's material, answered individually then in pairs. Retrieval, not recognition. |
| 25–60 min | **Lecture block 1** | One big idea, one case, one diagram. Never more than one new diagram per block. |
| 60–70 min | **Break** | Non-negotiable; protects attention for block 2. |
| 70–100 min | **Lecture block 2** | The model applied to the same case; show a recorded or live demonstration. |
| 100–120 min | **Whole-group debrief** | "What would you have done differently?" Collect on the board; do not resolve everything. |
| Practical hour | **Skills triad** | Observer / therapist / client, 12-minute rounds, structured feedback sheet, rotate. |

**The observer is not passive.** Give the observer a single narrow task per round — count the closed
questions, note every time the therapist interrupted, or track who spoke most. Narrow observation is
what makes feedback usable.

---

## 2. Assessment design principles

1. **Assess the skill, not the vocabulary.** A student who can define "enactment" and cannot conduct
   one should not pass the clinical component.
2. **Use unseen cases for exams and seen cases for assignments.** Seen cases teach; unseen cases test.
3. **Mark formulations on reasoning, not on agreement with your formulation.** The question is "is this
   hypothesis supported by the evidence in the case and testable?", not "is it the one I would have had?"
4. **Require risk documentation in every clinical assignment.** A formulation with no risk screen is
   incomplete, however elegant.

### Sample marking rubric for a case formulation (30%)

| Criterion | Weight | What excellence looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Systemic description | 20% | Circular, multi-person, includes context, no single-villain language |
| Evidence use | 20% | Claims traced to specific case detail; gaps named |
| Formulation quality | 25% | 5-P grid coherent; one clear focus; explicitly testable |
| Model application | 15% | Technique justified by formulation, not by preference |
| Ethics, culture and risk | 10% | Risk screen, power, culture and consent addressed |
| Writing and referencing | 10% | Plain professional prose; accurate APA-style referencing |

## 3. Teaching the difficult topics

**Suicide and self-harm (MFT 712).** Do not lecture about risk assessment without demonstrating one.
Use a scripted role-play with a colleague, then let students practise in triads with a checklist. Expect
anxiety; name it and use it.

**Intimate partner violence (MFT 704, 710, 712).** Never role-play a live violent couple in a
whole-group format without a clear stop signal and a pre-brief. Screen separately, always. Teach the
difference between *situational couple violence* and *coercive control* early, because the treatment
implications diverge sharply.

**Culture (MFT 708).** Avoid the "cultural knowledge" trap. Teach students to ask, not to assume, and to
locate themselves on the same dimensions they are assessing.

**Trauma (MFT 714).** Students with their own trauma histories will be activated by this material.
Announce content in advance, offer an opt-out route that is not stigmatising, and keep a referral
pathway for students as well as clients.

## 4. Running the skills labs

* **Groups of three, fixed for the semester.** Continuity produces trust; changing groups each week
  produces polite performances.
* **Twelve-minute rounds, three-minute feedback, rotate.** Protect the time strictly; over-running is
  the most common facilitation error.
* **Feedback sandwich is banned.** Use: "what I saw", "what it did to me as the client", "one thing to
  keep, one thing to try".
* **Record one round per student per month** (with consent) so they can see themselves rather than
  argue with feedback.
* **The lecturer circulates and joins rounds as a co-therapist**, which is more powerful modelling than
  any demonstration.

## 5. Common student difficulties (and what to do)

| Difficulty | What it looks like | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Advice-giving | Answers every problem with a solution | Ban "have you tried…" for one full session; require a question instead |
| Individual focus | Interviews one person while the family watches | Require every 5 minutes to include a different person's voice |
| Anxiety-driven over-structuring | Fills silence, runs a curriculum | Teach deliberate silence; debrief what the silence produced |
| Rescuing | Works harder than the family | Assign a session where the student does 30% less talking and reports what happened |
| Theory-reciting | Names models, cannot enact them | Move assessment from "explain" to "demonstrate" |
| Boundary blur | Becomes the family's friend | Review the case plan and the fee/contact policy together |

## 6. Materials, ethics and logistics

* **Recorded sessions:** obtain written consent from every adult participant and a guardian for minors,
  explain who will view the recording, and store it encrypted with a deletion date.
* **Live supervision:** brief the family in advance that a supervisor may call in; agree the signal the
  therapist can use to pause.
* **Role-play disclosure:** state at the start of the semester that personal material shared in skills
  practice is confidential within the group but that students may always decline to use their own
  experience.
* **Assessment security:** rotate unseen cases each year and keep the case bank in one place.

---

## 7. A suggested semester rhythm

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Orientation, contracting, baseline quiz, first skills triad |
| 2–5 | Core content blocks; formative case formulation in Week 4 (ungraded feedback) |
| 6 | Mid-semester skills assessment (pass/fail gate, resit available) |
| 7–10 | Application weeks; guest clinician or recorded session; assignment set in Week 8 |
| 11 | Assignment submission; peer review session |
| 12–13 | Integration weeks; students present their formulation to the group |
| 14 | Revision, seen-case walkthrough, exam briefing |
| 15 | Examination |
| 16 | Feedback, marks, progression meeting |

---

## 8. If you only have three hours with this book

Teach Week 1 (framing), the week containing the central model, and the integration chapter. Set the
practicals as self-directed triad work with a submitted log. That preserves the spine of the course:
what the work is, how it is done, and how it fits together.

## 9. Printing and distributing the materials

Every course is available as a finished A4 PDF in [`../pdf/`](../pdf/README.md); the whole programme is in
[`MFT-Library-Complete-28-volumes.pdf`](../pdf/MFT-Library-Complete-28-volumes.pdf). Practical notes for
running a semester from the print edition:

* **Set the reading from the page numbers, not the chapter titles.** Each PDF's contents page carries real
  page numbers, and the bookmarks in the sidebar match them, so "read pages 6–9 before Thursday" is
  unambiguous.
* **Print the week, not the book.** The weekly sections are self-contained: the idea, the practice, a case
  and a reflective task. A 14-week course prints to roughly 25 pages of core text.
* **Photocopy the practicals.** Callout panels (case, practical, caution, debate, reflection) are set in
  tinted boxes precisely so they can be lifted onto a worksheet without re-typing.
* **Licence.** CC BY 4.0: copy, adapt and translate freely with attribution. If you change a book, mark it
  as changed and keep the attribution line from the imprint page.
* **Regenerating.** After editing the Markdown, run `python3 scripts/build_pdfs.py --complete` and both the
  individual volumes and the merged file update, with contents and bookmarks rebuilt automatically.
