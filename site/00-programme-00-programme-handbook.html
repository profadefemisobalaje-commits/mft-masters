1. What this programme is

This is a professional, clinical, systemic master's programme. It trains graduates to work with couples, families, children and organisations as relationships rather than as collections of individuals. The programme therefore differs from a clinical psychology or counselling degree in three specific ways:

  1. The unit of treatment is the relationship. You will learn to assess and intervene in patterns between people — cycles, triangles, coalitions, boundaries — not only in the inner world of one person.
  2. The therapist is part of the system. From Week 1 you will be trained to notice your own effect on the room: your reactivity, your culture, your family-of-origin triggers, your use of power.
  3. Practice is relational and accountable. Supervision, ethics, law, and cultural humility are not add-on courses; they are the conditions under which the clinical work is allowed to happen.

The programme is designed for a 14–16 week semester at roughly 1.71 units per course. In practice, that means about 3 contact hours per week per course (a 2-hour lecture plus a 1-hour practical, skills lab, or supervision seminar), plus 4–6 hours of reading and practice between sessions.

4. Regulation-style essentials

4.1 Attendance and participation

Skills labs and supervision seminars cannot be made up by reading. A minimum of 75% attendance in practical sessions is normally required before a student may sit the clinical examination. Absence from a session in which a student would have role-played a client must be made up, because peers depend on one another to practise.

4.2 Assessment pattern (per course)

ComponentWeightNotes
Continuous assessment: weekly practical/reflective log20%Submitted fortnightly; not graded on insight but on consistency and specificity
Case formulation or applied assignment30%2,000–2,500 words, anonymised, with supervisor sign-off
End-of-semester written examination50%Two hours; seen-case + essay + short answers

The Master Thesis (MFT 770) is assessed separately: proposal (10%), ethics approval (pass/fail gate), thesis (70%), oral defence (20%).

4.3 Clinical placement

Most programmes require 300–500 supervised client contact hours across Semesters Two and Three, including a minimum of 100 relational (couple/family) hours. Log every session: date, modality, hours, presenting issue, risk screen completed (Y/N), supervision received. This log is a professional document — treat it as you would a clinical record.

4.4 Supervision requirement

A minimum ratio of 1 hour of supervision to 5 hours of client contact is the working norm, with at least one live or recorded observation per month. Students may not carry relational cases without a named supervisor.

4.5 Academic integrity and case material

Every case written for assessment must be disguised: change names, ages by a year or two, occupations, locations, family composition and any identifying detail. Never submit material that a reader could trace to a real family. Fabricating a case is a more serious offence than writing a weak one — write about a composite or a role-play if you have no suitable live case.

4.6 Fitness to practise

Clinical programmes carry a fitness-to-practise obligation. Persistent boundary problems, dishonesty in records, untreated impairment, or unsafe practice in role-play are addressed through a support-and- remediation process, not only through marks. Asking for help early is treated as professional behaviour, not weakness.

5. How the books fit together

The curriculum spirals rather than stacks. The same case — the Adeyemi family, used in several books — is visited repeatedly at increasing depth:

BookWhat you do with the Adeyemi family
701Map the system; identify the referral question
702Apply three different schools to the same family
706Practise joining and circular questioning in role-play
707Complete a multi-informant assessment and 5-P formulation
710Work the couple's pursue–withdraw cycle
711Meet Amara individually without losing the system
715Redraw the structure and communication patterns
717Consider differential diagnosis and medication interface
719Locate the family at a stage transition and plan for the next

Using one family across courses is deliberate: it teaches you that models are lenses, not truths, and that a real family does not arrive labelled with a theory.

6. Reading strategy

Three tiers of reading are marked in each book:

  • Core (must read before the session). Roughly 20–30 pages per week.
  • Applied (read one). A case study, manual chapter or paper that shows the model in action.
  • Critical (read before the assignment). A critique, meta-analysis or culturally situated paper.

If you are behind, read the core tier and the week's practical, and borrow notes for the rest. Do not skip the practicals; they are where the learning is consolidated.

8. A closing word to the student

You are training to sit with people at the worst moments of their lives — a disclosure of betrayal, a child who will not speak, a family deciding whether to stay together, a death that has just happened. Technique matters, and this programme will give you plenty. But the thing that will make you safe and useful is simpler: the capacity to stay calm, curious and kind while another person's distress is in the room, and to know honestly what you do not know.

That capacity is built slowly, in supervision, in your own therapy, in your family, and in the ordinary discipline of showing up. Start now.
