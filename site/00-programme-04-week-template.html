The teaching-week template

Every teaching week in every course book is written to a single standard: not less than 8,000 words of week content, delivered through the same thirteen components so a student always knows where to find what. The checker (scripts/check_week_lengths.py) measures each week from its heading to the next ## heading, and fails the build if any week falls short.

House style for week content

  • Headings: ### 7.1, ### 7.2 … under the week's ## heading, so the PDF contents nests correctly.
  • Callouts are blockquote panels, one per line start: > CASE —, > PRACTICAL —, > CAUTION —, > DEBATE —, > REFLECT —, > DRILL —, > TASK —, > AUDIT —. Dialogue is written as > Speaker: … lines.
  • Cases are fictional composites. Names recur across the library so students meet the same families in different courses: the Adeyemi family, Mrs Okonkwo and Ebuka (9), Folake and Dele, Aisha and Sam.
  • Every clinical claim that has an evidence base gets a hedge: robust, plausible, popular but unsupported. Students are being trained to read, not to believe.
  • Currency: all figures cited as a percentage must be internally consistent within the book; where a figure is illustrative rather than sourced, say so.
  • No week may end without a reflective task and a reading list.

Word-count discipline. Prose is not padded. The word count is met by covering the topic properly: the history of the idea, the mechanism, the clinical procedure, the failure modes, the cultural application, the evidence, the assessment. A week that reaches 8,000 words by repetition is a failed week; a week that reaches 8,000 words because the topic needed it is the standard.
