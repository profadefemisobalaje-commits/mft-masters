# MFT 770 — Master Thesis

> **MSc Marriage and Family Therapy** · Semester Three (continuing) · 1.71 Units · 14 structured weeks + submission and defence · Prerequisite: MFT 705, 721

**Course leader's summary.** The thesis is not a bigger essay. It is a supervised, ethical, original inquiry
that a professional body would accept as evidence that you can generate knowledge, not only consume it. This
book is the project management manual plus the academic guide.

---

## How to use this book

Work the milestones in order; they are dated to protect you from the two classic disasters — a brilliant plan
that cannot be completed, and a completed study that answers nothing. Bring the weekly artefact to supervision
even if it is imperfect. Progress beats polish.

---

## 1. What the thesis is

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Length | 15,000–20,000 words (excluding appendices), or the institutional equivalent |
| Contribution | Original, however modest: new data, new analysis, a local validation, a qualitative account, a service evaluation |
| Methods | Quantitative, qualitative or mixed; must be defensible and feasible |
| Ethics | Institutional approval before any data collection |
| Supervision | Minimum fortnightly contact with a named supervisor |
| Output | Bound thesis, oral defence, and (encouraged) a publishable manuscript |

**What it is not.** A literature review alone (that is a different degree component). A clinical case series
without a systematic method. A replication of a manual with no question. A project that requires resources you
do not have.

## 2. The fourteen structured weeks

| Week | Milestone | Artefact due in supervision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Topic refinement and scope | One-page problem statement |
| 2 | Literature search strategy | Search log and 20 key references |
| 3 | Critical literature review draft | 2,000-word draft |
| 4 | Research question and aims | PICO/SPIDER and aims table |
| 5 | Design selection | Design decision log |
| 6 | Measurement and materials | Instrument pack |
| 7 | Ethics application | Completed ethics form |
| 8 | Sampling and recruitment plan | Recruitment protocol and letters |
| 9 | Analysis plan | Pre-specified analysis plan |
| 10 | Pilot or feasibility work | Pilot findings and amendments |
| 11 | Data collection checkpoint | Recruitment and data quality report |
| 12 | Analysis progress | First results tables/coding frame |
| 13 | Findings and discussion drafting | Draft chapters 4–5 |
| 14 | Full draft and defence preparation | Complete draft, viva questions |

**Timeline discipline.** A part-time student should assume 12–18 months from registration to defence. Full-time
students commonly need 9–12 months. If your plan assumes everything goes right, add 30%.

---

## 3. Choosing a question you can finish

**The three-circle test.** A viable thesis sits where (1) you are curious, (2) the field needs it, and (3) you
have access. If any circle is missing, choose a different question, not a bigger effort.

**Good thesis questions in this field (examples).**

1. What is the reliability and factor structure of the RDAS in a Nigerian clinical sample?
2. How do fathers describe their experience of being excluded from family therapy?
3. Does a four-session psychoeducation group reduce caregiver burden among families of people with psychosis?
4. What do couples report about the process of rebuilding trust after infidelity?
5. What predicts drop-out after a first family therapy appointment in a community clinic?
6. How do therapists in training describe the influence of their own family of origin on their clinical
   decisions?
7. Does a culturally adapted version of the speaker–listener technique improve communication in a small
   clinical series?

**Questions to avoid.** "The effect of family therapy on family functioning" (too broad); "Why is family
therapy better than individual therapy?" (advocacy, not inquiry); anything requiring a sample you cannot access
or an instrument you cannot obtain.

> **ARTEFACT** — A one-page problem statement: what is unknown, why it matters, who it affects, and what you
> will produce.

## 4. The literature review

**Purpose.** Not to summarise everything, but to establish (a) what is known, (b) what is contested, (c) what is
missing, and (d) why your question follows.

**Search discipline.**

- At least three databases (PubMed, PsycINFO, Scopus, AJOL, Google Scholar as a supplement).
- Document the strategy: terms, limits, date, number of results, number retained.
- Use a reference manager from day one.
- Read systematic reviews first; they map the field faster than primary papers.

**Structure of the chapter.** Context → key constructs and definitions → what the evidence shows → what is
contested → what is missing (the gap) → your question and aims.

**The gap must be specific.** "Few studies have examined X in Y population using Z method" — not "more research
is needed."

---

## 5. Design and methods

**The rule.** The method chapter must be detailed enough that a competent reader could replicate the study,
and honest enough that they could see its limits.

**Chapter contents.**

1. Design and rationale.
2. Setting and context.
3. Participants: inclusion, exclusion, recruitment, consent, sample size justification.
4. Measures: source, psychometrics, scoring, adaptation and translation procedures.
5. Procedure: what happened, in what order, for how long.
6. Analysis: software, models, assumptions, missing data, coding approach, rigour procedures.
7. Ethical considerations: approval reference, consent, confidentiality, risk, data storage.
8. Researcher position and reflexivity.

---

## 6. Ethics

**Required before any data collection.** A retrospective application is a serious breach.

**Standard components.** Purpose and design; participants and recruitment; consent process and documents;
risks and benefits; confidentiality and data management; payment; dissemination; and procedures for distress or
incidental findings.

**Family-specific issues.** Consent from guardians plus assent from minors; separate consent for each family
member; managing the possibility that one member withdraws; protecting group confidentiality; and a protocol
for disclosures of risk during data collection.

> **ARTEFACT** — A complete ethics application with consent forms for an adult and a minor, and a risk
> protocol for a participant who discloses harm during an interview.

---

## 7. Data collection

**Pilot first.** Five to ten participants will reveal problems that a hundred will not forgive: unclear
questions, unworkable recruitment, unusable audio, unrealistic time estimates.

**Data quality.** Check recordings the same week; transcribe promptly; keep a fieldwork log; record refusals and
reasons (they are data).

**Recruitment tactics that work in clinical settings.** A named contact who can introduce you; a one-page
information sheet; flexible times; a clear statement of what participation involves; and a follow-up call at 48
hours. Do not rely on posters.

---

## 8. Analysis

**Quantitative.** Follow the pre-specified plan; document deviations; check assumptions; report descriptives
before inference; report effect sizes with confidence intervals; run sensitivity analyses; keep the analysis log
current.

**Qualitative.** Transcribe verbatim; code systematically; keep memos; audit your coding; discuss divergence with
a second coder; move from codes to themes with an explicit, documented logic; and present themes with data
extracts (anonymised).

**Mixed methods.** Decide in advance how the strands will be integrated, and produce at least one joint display.

---

## 9. Writing the thesis

**Standard structure.**

| Chapter | Content | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and background | 2,000 |
| 2 | Literature review | 5,000 |
| 3 | Methodology | 3,500 |
| 4 | Findings | 4,000 |
| 5 | Discussion | 3,500 |
| 6 | Conclusion and recommendations | 1,000 |
| — | References, appendices | — |

**Writing rules.**

- Write the method first; it is the easiest chapter and it clarifies everything.
- Draft findings as soon as you have data; do not wait for "enough".
- Keep a "parking lot" file for ideas that belong elsewhere.
- Write 500 words a day rather than 5,000 in a panic.
- Read your work aloud; if you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.

**Voice.** Academic but human. First person is acceptable in reflective and qualitative sections; follow your
institution's guidance. Avoid both inflated jargon and chatty informality.

## 10. The supervision relationship

**What your supervisor owes you.** Regular contact; timely feedback; methodological and ethical guidance;
honesty about the standard of your work; and reasonable availability.

**What you owe your supervisor.** Prepared meetings with an agenda and an artefact; drafts submitted on time;
decisions recorded; and honesty about difficulties — especially delays.

**Managing difficulty.** If supervision is not working, raise it early with the programme director. A change of
supervisor is legitimate and common; drifting for six months is not.

**The meeting agenda template.**

1. Progress since last meeting (three bullets).
2. Decisions needed today (the most important item first).
3. Problems I am stuck on.
4. What I will do before the next meeting.
5. Date of next meeting.

---

## 11. The viva

**What examiners test.** Whether you understand your own study: why you chose it, what you did, what it means,
and what it cannot support.

**Preparation.**

1. Re-read your thesis and mark every claim you could not defend.
2. Prepare a two-minute summary.
3. Prepare answers to the four standard questions: design, validity, analysis, conclusion.
4. Rehearse with a peer who is instructed to be difficult.
5. Bring a copy of your thesis, your analysis log and a pen.

**In the room.** Answer the question asked. Say "I don't know" when you don't. Distinguish what the data show
from what you hope they show. Correct errors gracefully.

**Common reasons for referral or failure.** Design cannot answer the question; analysis misapplied; ethics
breach; unacknowledged limitations; and evidence of work not being the candidate's own.

---

## 12. After the defence

**Corrections.** Make them promptly, with a tracking table mapping each examiner comment to the change made.

**Dissemination.** Submit a manuscript to a journal; present at a conference; give a plain-language summary to
the participating service. Dissemination is part of the ethical contract with your participants.

**Your data.** Retain as required by your institution's policy, then destroy securely.

---

## 13. Project management

| Risk | Early warning | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment failure | Fewer than 50% of target by mid-point | Widen eligibility; add sites; adjust design (with supervisor and ethics approval) |
| Analysis paralysis | Repeated re-coding without writing | Fixed decision date; write the methods and limitations |
| Supervisor unavailability | Missed meetings | Written agenda; escalate early |
| Personal crisis | Missed deadlines | Tell the programme early; request extension with evidence |
| Scope creep | New questions each week | Freeze the question after Week 4; park ideas |

**The one-page dashboard.** Question, milestones, current position, next action, date, supervisor appointment.
Update weekly; it will save your degree.

---

## 14. Assessment

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Proposal (approved by panel) | 10% |
| Ethics approval | Pass/fail gate |
| Thesis | 70% |
| Oral defence | 20% |

**Marking criteria (typical).** Originality and significance; command of the literature; appropriateness and
rigour of method; quality of analysis; critical discussion; ethical practice; presentation and referencing.

## Appendix: thesis checklist

- [ ] Question is specific, answerable, and stated in one sentence
- [ ] Literature search documented and reproducible
- [ ] Gap statement is specific, not "more research is needed"
- [ ] Design justified against at least one alternative
- [ ] Sample size justification provided
- [ ] Ethics reference number included
- [ ] Measures described with psychometric properties and any adaptation
- [ ] Analysis plan pre-specified; deviations recorded and explained
- [ ] Assumptions checked and reported
- [ ] Effect sizes with confidence intervals
- [ ] Findings presented before interpretation
- [ ] Limitations specific and honest
- [ ] Implications for practice are actionable
- [ ] Referencing consistent and complete
- [ ] Plain-language summary prepared for participants
